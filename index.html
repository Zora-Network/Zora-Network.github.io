A closer look

Zora Network.

Zora Network is a layer-2 built on the OP Stack that treats minting, collecting and sharing onchain media as everyday actions instead of expensive ones. It inherits Ethereum's security while running as part of the Optimism Superchain, which keeps transaction costs low enough that a single post, image or song can become a tradable onchain object without a meaningful gas bill. Gas is paid in ETH, creators and collectors interact through the same wallets they already use, and the network's protocol rewards route a share of every mint back to the people who made and shared the work.

What Is Zora Network

Zora Network is an Ethereum layer-2 built on the OP Stack specifically for creators, collectors and onchain media, where minting a post, image or piece of music is cheap enough to do casually. Rather than positioning itself as a general-purpose chain for every kind of app, Zora Network narrows its focus to the act of turning content into an onchain object anyone can own, trade or build on top of. That focus shapes everything from its fee design to the protocol-level rewards that pay creators automatically whenever their work is minted or referred further.

Built on the OP Stack

The network runs on the OP Stack, the open-source rollup framework also used by Optimism and several other chains, which handles execution off Ethereum while still posting the data needed to reconstruct the chain's state back to Ethereum. Using a shared, audited stack means it benefits from the same rollup security model and the same Optimism documentation that describes how OP Stack chains settle and finalize.

Part of the Superchain

It is also a member of the broader Superchain, a group of OP Stack chains designed to share standards, security upgrades and, eventually, liquidity and messaging. Belonging to that group matters practically: it is part of why tools, bridges and wallets that already support one OP Stack chain tend to support this one with little extra work.

ETH stays the gas token

Unlike some layer-2s that introduce a separate gas token, this chain keeps ETH as the asset you spend on every transaction. That choice removes a step for newcomers — there is no second token to acquire before you can mint, collect or transfer — and it keeps the fee market anchored to the same asset the rest of the Ethereum ecosystem already holds.

The ZORA Token and Protocol Rewards on Zora Network

ZORA is the network's native token, layered on top of the ETH-denominated gas model rather than replacing it. Protocol rewards are the more distinctive mechanic: every time content is minted, a portion of that mint's value is split automatically between the creator, the collector who referred the sale, and the platform that surfaced it, settled onchain without a separate claim process. That structure turns sharing into an incentive rather than an afterthought — a collector who posts a link that leads to more mints earns a cut of them, and the creator is paid regardless of which app or feed the mint happened through, since the reward logic lives in the protocol and not in any single interface.

Minting and Creating on Zora Network

Minting on the network turns a piece of content — an image, a video, a song, a post — into an onchain token that a collector can hold, trade or display, and the process is deliberately close to posting on a regular app. A creator uploads the content, sets terms such as price and supply, and the mint contract handles the rest.

What creators control

Creators decide whether a mint is free or priced, time-limited or open-ended, and whether it has a fixed supply or stays open to new collectors indefinitely. Because these settings live in the contract rather than in a platform's backend, the same mint behaves identically no matter which front end a collector uses to view it.

What collectors receive

A collector who mints receives an onchain token tied to that specific piece of content, viewable in any wallet or marketplace that reads the chain, and tradable afterward the same way any other NFT is. The low cost of minting means collecting is not reserved for high-value pieces — it works just as well for everyday posts as for limited editions.

Fees and Transaction Speed

Because the network posts its transaction data through the OP Stack's rollup design rather than writing everything directly to Ethereum mainnet, everyday activity settles quickly and at a small fraction of mainnet cost. Creators, Collectors and the Zora Network Ecosystem

This ecosystem is built around a simple loop: creators mint, collectors discover and collect, and protocol rewards pay both sides for participating. A handful of patterns recur across the apps and tools built on top of it.
  • Social and media apps that let anyone turn a post directly into a mintable onchain collectible.
  • Marketplaces and explorers where collectors browse, buy and resell items minted on the network.
  • Referral-driven discovery, where sharing a mint link earns a reward if it leads to further mints.
  • Creator storefronts that bundle multiple pieces of work into one onchain profile.
  • Developer tooling for building new mint formats on top of the same underlying contracts.

Security and Bridging to Zora Network

Moving assets onto the network follows the same pattern as other OP Stack chains: you keep your own keys the entire time, and the bridge contract — not a custodian — holds the deposited funds until they are credited on the other side.

How bridging works

A deposit is confirmed on Ethereum and then credited on the rollup within minutes; a withdrawal has to wait out the network's challenge window before funds release back on Ethereum, which is the standard security delay on any OP Stack rollup rather than something specific to this chain. You can verify any transaction's status directly on the Zora explorer.

What to check before you sign

Confirm the contract address a mint or bridge transaction points to, keep approvals limited to the amount you intend to spend, and never share your seed phrase — no legitimate app on this network will ever ask for it. The Zora docs walk through the exact steps for minting, collecting and bridging if you want the reference version of any flow described here.

How It Compares to Other Layer-2s

General-purpose layer-2s optimize for breadth — supporting any kind of app with no particular lean toward content or creators. It instead optimizes for one job: making it cheap and fast to turn media into something ownable. That narrower focus shows up in protocol rewards, which are not a generic feature of layer-2 scaling but a network-specific mechanic built directly into how mints settle. If you want a chain for creator and NFT activity specifically, that focus is the main reason to pick Zora Network over a broader L2; if you need a chain for unrelated DeFi or gaming activity, a more general-purpose rollup — reviewed independently on L2BEAT — may fit better.

Questions & answers

What is Zora Network?
It is an Ethereum layer-2 built on the OP Stack and focused on creators, NFTs and onchain media, where minting content costs a small ETH fee instead of full mainnet gas.
What gas token does Zora Network use?
It uses ETH for gas, the same asset used across Ethereum and most other OP Stack chains, so there is no separate gas token to acquire.
Is Zora Network part of the Superchain?
Yes. It is an OP Stack chain and a member of the Superchain, the group of rollups that share standards and security upgrades with Optimism.
What is the ZORA token used for?
ZORA is the network's native token. It sits alongside the ETH-denominated gas model and is distinct from protocol rewards, which pay creators and referrers automatically whenever content is minted.
How do protocol rewards work on Zora Network?
Every mint splits a share of its value between the creator, the collector who referred the sale, and the platform that surfaced it, settled onchain at the moment of the mint.
How do I bridge assets to Zora Network?
You deposit through the canonical bridge from Ethereum, which credits your balance within minutes; withdrawals take longer because they wait out the rollup's challenge window before releasing funds back on Ethereum.
Who uses Zora Network?
Creators who want to mint media as onchain collectibles, collectors who discover and trade that work, and developers building social, marketplace or referral tools on top of the same mint contracts.
